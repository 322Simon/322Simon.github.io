Most of my projects circle one problem: reading what a person intends from their body (muscle signals, movement, posture, gait, gaze) and making it usable for a machine.

## Hand gestures from muscle signals

*M.Sc. thesis · ETH Zurich, Biomedical and Mobile Health Technology Lab · 2026 – 2027, in progress*

When you move your fingers, the muscles in your forearm produce electrical activity that a grid of electrodes on the skin can record: high-density surface EMG. My thesis compares machine-learning algorithms that decode hand gestures from these recordings.

Supervised by Prof. Carlo Menon (ETH Zurich), in collaboration with Prof. Moe Elgendi (Khalifa University).

## Two sides of a handover

*B.Sc. thesis and research at TUM · 2024 – 2025*

A handover has two sides. What does the person do, given how the robot moves? And how should the robot move so the person gets it right? To hold an object securely you scale your grip force to its weight, ideally before you feel it. In a virtual grasping setup, each object's weight was signalled by a cue, either a colour or the robot's movement, and I observed how participants adapted their grip force to it. My bachelor's thesis looked at predicting object weight from these kinematic cues.

At TUM's robotics institute (MIRMI) I then ran experiments with 32 participants on predictive mechanisms in simulated and physical robot–human handovers. The resulting paper is under review.

## How much information fits in your gaze?

*Research project, eye tracking · 2026*

When what you're looking at packs in more information, how does the eye adapt? In eye-tracking data from 16 participants, higher local information density led to a shorter horizontal gaze span: visual sampling narrows as complexity rises. Gaze "chunks" also ended at character changes rather than at random places. That suggests the visual system splits its input into meaningful units, pacing the flow of information into working memory so it doesn't overload.

## Movement control with haptic robots

*TUM, Chair for Neuromuscular Diagnostics · 2024 – 2025*

A year of experiments on the physiological and computational principles of human movement control, using vBot and 3D-Phantom haptic robots. I built the Python pipeline for preparing, analysing and visualising the data.
